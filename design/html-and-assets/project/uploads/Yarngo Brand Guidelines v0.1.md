# Yarngo Brand Guidelines v0.1

## 1. Brand

**Name:** Yarngo  
**Pronunciation:** yarn-go  
**Wordmark:** `yarngo`

Yarngo begins with the Nigerian expression *yarn*, meaning to talk, tell, converse or gist, combined with *go*, suggesting movement and action.

The origin is Nigerian. The ambition is global.

Yarngo should not present itself as a Nigerian product trying to look international. It should present itself as a global voice company that was built from a part of the world voice technology historically understood poorly.

## 2. Brand idea

**Voice AI for the way people actually speak.**

Yarngo enables people to create, hear and interact with natural voice experiences across languages, accents and ways of speaking.

The brand should make advanced speech technology feel human, expressive and culturally aware rather than clinical.

## 3. Brand promise

**Speak naturally. Everywhere.**

Yarngo should help a person sound like themselves, communicate in the language they want and move naturally between text, speech and conversation.

## 4. Positioning

For people and businesses that want voice technology to understand how people actually speak, Yarngo is a multilingual voice AI platform for creating, distributing and interacting through voice.

Unlike speech products designed primarily around dominant global accents and languages, Yarngo begins with linguistic diversity as a core product requirement.

Nigeria is the starting point, not the boundary.

## 5. Product architecture

### Yarngo Create

Generate, translate, preview and share speech.

### Yarngo Channels

Subscribe to useful, entertaining or informative voice programming.

### Yarngo Chat

Private AI conversations through text and voice.

### Yarngo Voice

The cloud voice layer exposed across Yarngo products.

### Yarngo API

Speech and language infrastructure for developers and businesses.

### Yarngo Studio

Future professional workspace for creators, publishers and businesses.

## 6. Naming

The canonical brand name is:

**Yarngo**

Use `yarngo` for the visual wordmark.

Avoid:

- YARNGO
- Yarn Go
- Yarn-Go
- YarNGo
- excessive emphasis on the internal `yarn + go` construction

Nigerian users can naturally discover the linguistic meaning. Global users only need to remember Yarngo as a brand.

The name should become larger than its etymology.

## 7. Tagline

Primary:

**Speak naturally. Everywhere.**

Supporting brand line:

**Voice AI for the way people actually speak.**

Campaign language can be more playful and locally specific.

Examples:

**Nigeria**
- Yarn am.
- Make your voice go.
- Talk your talk.
- However you yarn, Yarngo gets it.

**Global**
- Find your voice.
- Make yourself heard.
- Your words, in your voice.
- Every voice has somewhere to go.

Do not make mango jokes or fruit references central to the master brand.

The mango-like sound is a useful source of warmth and memorability, not the product proposition.

## 8. Personality

Yarngo is:

**Human**  
Technology should disappear behind conversation.

**Confident**  
The brand understands the cultures and languages it supports.

**Expressive**  
Voice communicates more than words.

**Curious**  
People speak differently. Yarngo treats that variation as valuable.

**Culturally fluent**  
Local language should feel natural, not manufactured.

**Playful when appropriate**  
Consumer experiences may use wit and rhythm.

Yarngo is not:

- robotic
- corporate
- patronizing
- excessively slang-heavy
- stereotypically "African"
- futuristic for the sake of appearing technological
- an imitation of Silicon Valley AI branding

## 9. Voice and tone

### Default voice

Short, clear and conversational.

Prefer:

> Your voice is ready.

Instead of:

> Your requested audio generation has successfully completed.

Prefer:

> Try another voice.

Instead of:

> Select an alternative voice configuration.

### Nigerian English and Pidgin

Use naturally where the audience and context justify it.

Good:

> Wetin you wan yarn?

Bad:

> My guy!!! Yarn am make we dey goooo!!!

Do not perform Nigerian identity through exaggerated slang.

### Global audiences

Use clear international English without removing the personality of the brand.

Yarngo should feel culturally specific in origin while remaining understandable anywhere.

## 10. Visual identity

### Primary colour

**Yarngo Orange**  
`#FF8A1F`

Represents energy, voice, movement and warmth.

It can subtly evoke mango without turning the identity into a fruit brand.

### Core neutrals

**Yarngo Ink**  
`#171717`

Primary text, dark surfaces and wordmark.

**Warm White**  
`#FFF9F2`

Primary light background.

### Secondary colours

**Leaf**  
`#287A57`

Signals language, growth and cultural depth.

**Signal Blue**  
`#596DF8`

Used sparingly for interactive, technical and developer-oriented experiences.

### Rules

Orange is an accent and brand field, not the default colour for long text.

Use Ink for primary typography.

Prefer Warm White to sterile pure white for branded surfaces.

Do not create rainbow interfaces where every language receives an arbitrary flag-like colour.

## 11. Typography

### Brand and headings

**Sora**

Geometric, contemporary and friendly.

### Product and body

**Noto Sans**

Use Noto Sans as the multilingual workhorse because language coverage matters more than typographic novelty.

The product must render supported diacritics correctly.

Typography must never require users to remove tone marks or alter spelling to fit the visual system.

### Developer surfaces

Use a high-quality monospace font for code and API documentation, with Noto Sans retained for explanatory text.

## 12. Logo direction

The primary identity should be a custom lowercase:

**yarngo**

The wordmark should feel rounded, fast and conversational.

Potential distinguishing details:

- a subtle forward movement in the `g`
- an `o` that can become a listening pulse
- soft terminals rather than mechanical geometry
- a small sound-wave or speech-motion gesture integrated into the letterforms

Avoid placing a generic microphone beside the name.

Avoid using a literal mango as the primary logo.

A mango/speech-bubble interpretation can exist as an illustration, mascot or campaign device without becoming the corporate mark.

## 13. Brand icon

The standalone icon should emerge from the wordmark rather than introducing a separate concept.

Preferred direction:

**the Yarngo `o` as a voice aperture**

States:

Listening  
Generating  
Speaking  
Ready

The same object can animate across the product.

This creates one recognizable visual primitive for app icon, loading state, microphone state and generated-audio playback.

## 14. Motion

Yarngo should feel alive when speech is present.

Motion vocabulary:

- pulse
- ripple
- breathe
- travel
- respond

Avoid aggressive equalizer animations and fake audio waveforms that do not correspond to actual state.

Motion should communicate:

> I'm listening.  
> I'm thinking.  
> I'm speaking.  
> I'm done.

## 15. Sound identity

Yarngo should eventually have a short sonic mark.

Direction:

Three notes.

1. acknowledgement
2. movement
3. resolution

It should sound warm and percussive rather than synthetic or cinematic.

Do not make the sonic identity dependent on one Nigerian musical tradition. Yarngo must eventually work across cultures.

## 16. Photography

Prioritize people speaking, listening, creating and sharing.

Show:

- real environments
- close human moments
- creators
- headphones and phones when relevant
- multiple generations
- urban and non-urban contexts
- culturally specific environments without turning them into scenery

Avoid:

- robot heads
- glowing AI brains
- generic waveform stock photography
- African maps
- national flags as shorthand for language
- staged "diversity" photography without product context

## 17. Illustration

Illustration should be bold, simple and rhythmic.

Use abstract forms inspired by:

- speech
- vibration
- conversation
- movement
- repetition
- call and response

The mango shape may occasionally appear as an Easter egg or playful brand motif.

It should never become the entire identity.

## 18. Product language

### Create

**What do you want to say?**

**Choose a voice**

**Generate voice**

**Ready to share**

### Channels

**Listen to what matters to you.**

**Follow channel**

**New episode**

### Chat

**Start a conversation**

**Text**

**Talk**

**Hear reply**

### Credits

Use the plain term:

**Yarngo Credits**

Do not invent a confusing monetary metaphor.

Always show approximately what a credit purchase enables before checkout.

Example:

> 300 credits  
> About 10 minutes of standard cloud voice

## 19. AI disclosure

Generated speech must never pretend to be human-generated when that distinction matters.

Use:

**AI-generated voice**

rather than defensive or technical wording.

Companion experiences must consistently communicate that the character is AI.

Trust should be part of the product identity, not compliance copy hidden in settings.

## 20. Brand hierarchy

**Yarngo** is always the master brand.

Preferred:

Yarngo Create  
Yarngo Channels  
Yarngo Chat  
Yarngo Voice  
Yarngo API  
Yarngo Studio

Avoid creating independent logos for every feature.

The master brand should accumulate recognition.

## 21. International expansion

Do not redesign Yarngo every time another country launches.

The system should accommodate:

- different scripts
- longer translations
- right-to-left interfaces
- local voice personalities
- local editorial channels
- different payment conventions

Local culture belongs in content and product experience.

The core Yarngo identity stays consistent.

## 22. Brand test

Every Yarngo experience should pass four questions:

**Does it sound human?**

**Does it respect how the user actually speaks?**

**Is it obvious what the technology is doing?**

**Could this still feel like Yarngo outside Nigeria?**

If the answer to any is no, revise it.

## 23. One-line identity

**Yarngo is voice AI for the way the world actually speaks.**
