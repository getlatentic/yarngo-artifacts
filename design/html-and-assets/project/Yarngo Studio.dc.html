4Clips-only sidebar, a fixed frame that does not move, and voice as a property of the clip

The left side lists clips and nothing else. A voice is not something you browse next to your work — it is a property of the clip you are making, so it moves right, next to the model and the delivery settings that belong to the same clip. The inspector is a toggle in the title bar; closed, the voice and model sit in the composer header as two chips that open it.

Every state uses one frame so nothing moves as you work: a fixed header, one card, one row of actions. What changes happens inside the card — a player strip appears at its top, the text locks, a progress bar runs — and the buttons underneath stay where they were. Clip rows are a fixed height for the same reason, so a clip that starts generating shows a progress line inside its own row instead of pushing the list down. Empty is 4a, writing is 4b, generating is 4c, a finished clip is 4d.

A new clip and an existing clip are still different screens: a field with Generate under it, against a player first with the text below as the record of what produced it. Names are yours — a clip takes its first words as a working name and 4e renames from the header or the row, with the audio and text untouched.

Nothing has to be recorded to generate. The bundled default voice is selected out of the box and works with every model; a voice you record is a reference, which only some models can use. So the inspector groups them — default first, yours below, the model's capability stated under the model name — and 4g sets the three cases side by side. Recording starts from that panel: 4h is the same sheet as first-run setup, so 2c and 2d remain its recording and check states, and saving switches the clip you were writing over to your voice (4i).

3No runtime, no model, and what the two title-bar controls open

The three things you can skip fail differently, so they should not look alike. A missing voice leaves a working app — 2a. A missing model leaves an app that can accept work but not do it yet, so 3b takes the text you typed and queues it: the download finishes, the clip generates, nothing is retyped. A missing runtime leaves nothing to do at all, so 3a is the only case that refuses to open the workspace — it returns to step 1 and states what is blocked, with a resume, an offline installer, and a copyable link for a machine that has no connection.

The pill and the gear split by frequency. The pill is a switcher — 3c lists what is on disk, switches on click, and shows what a switch costs in seconds and memory. Everything slower than that lives behind the gear — 3d, a normal settings window where models are added and deleted, disk is accounted for, and the runtime version and update channel are visible. Deleting a model names the voices that stop working.

2Skipping the voice, adding it later, and a script you read instead of type

Skipping step 3 leaves the app in a real state, not a broken one. 2a keeps the composer usable — you can type, and you can hear something immediately through a bundled sample voice — but Generate stays off until a voice exists, and the reason sits next to the button rather than in an alert. The sample is labelled everywhere it is used, so nobody ships a clip thinking it was theirs.

Adding a voice later is the same job as step 3, so it is the same layout in a sheet over the workspace — 2b to 2d. One recorder, reached from setup, from the sidebar, or from the empty composer.

On the fixed script: if the words are given, the panel should not look like a field you can type in. So the script becomes the large left panel set in reading type with a lock on its label, and the recorder — device, button, level, timer — moves to a narrow right column. Reading is the task, so it gets the space; 2c follows the sentence you are on, and 2d checks the take against the script you were given. An escape hatch stays for people who want their own words.

1Yarngo Studio — install, add a voice, generate

A desktop app that speaks in your own voice, with the models on your machine. The current build puts everything in one column: model chips, a voice button, a box, Generate. That works once you are set up, but it says nothing about the two things a first-time user has to do — get a runtime and a model onto disk, and record a reference clip with the text that matches it.

So the app has two modes. Setup is a three-step wizard shown once, because a download is a wait with a size and a failure case, and the model choice deserves the licence and disk cost next to the name instead of five chips. After that it is a workspace: voices and clips on the left, one composer on the right. Model selection moves into the title bar as status, since it is a setting you pick rarely and want to see always.

Two rules carry through. Reference audio and reference text are one object — a voice is not saved until both exist, which is why 1c checks them together rather than accepting a clip and asking for the transcript later. Nothing is silently remote: the footer states where generation happens, and setup states what is written to disk and where.
